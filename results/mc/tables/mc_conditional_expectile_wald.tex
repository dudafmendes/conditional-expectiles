\textit{Notes:} The target is $c_{n+1}=\sigma_{n+1}(\bs\theta_0)\xi_0$ and its estimator is $\widehat c_{n+1}=\widetilde\sigma_{n+1}(\widehat{\bs\theta}_n)\widehat\xi_n$. For replication $r$, define $e_r=\widehat c_{n+1,r}-c_{n+1,r}$. The corresponding studentized statistic is $Z_{c,n+1,r}=\sqrt n\,e_r/\sqrt{\widehat V_{n+1,r}}$. The number of Monte Carlo replications is $R=10{,}000$. Bias is $R^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{R}e_r$, and RMSE is $\{R^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{R}e_r^2\}^{1/2}$. SD, Skew., and Ex. kurt. are, respectively, the sample standard deviation and the empirical standardized third and fourth central moments of $Z_{c,n+1}$; 3 is subtracted from the fourth moment. The standard Normal reference values are therefore 1, 0, and 0. Cov. is the fraction of replications satisfying $|Z_{c,n+1}|\leq 1.96$, equivalently the empirical coverage of the nominal 95\% two-sided Wald interval. Wald-statistic moments and coverage are computed over replications with a finite, strictly positive estimated asymptotic variance; all 10,000 replications satisfy this requirement in every reported design. The expectile level is $\tau=0.95$. Low and high persistence correspond to $p_0=0.90$ and $p_0=0.98$, respectively. Student-$t$ innovations are standardized to unit variance. Bias and RMSE are reported in the units of the corresponding risk measure.
